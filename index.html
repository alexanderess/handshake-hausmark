Pillar 9 · Escrow payment

Why the way you pay matters as much as who you hire.

A renovation runs on money paid for work that does not exist yet. Deposits of 30%, 40%, even 50% before anyone lifts a hammer are still common in Singapore — and Handshake's own guidance is blunt about it: for a full home renovation there is no legitimate reason for a deposit above 20%, and a fair structure runs four to five stages with 10–15% upfront. Until each stage is actually done, you are ahead of your contractor, funding work nobody has performed. That is fine right up until it isn't. If the firm stalls, overruns or winds up, the money has already left your account and you join the queue of people trying to get it back.

Renovation insurance does not close that gap. Deposit loss, contractor default, payment disputes and incomplete works fall outside what renovation insurance products in Singapore cover — those are financial risks, not accidents. A regulated payment escrow is the instrument built for exactly this.

Escrow changes the order without changing the deal. You commit the funds, so your ID has certainty they will be paid and can confidently order materials and book trades. But the money sits in a regulated account rather than in the firm's, and is released stage by stage as you approve work that is genuinely finished. Neither side is holding the other's money. Your ID stops chasing payment, and you stop paying for a kitchen you cannot yet see.

Handshake Renovation Safeguard

Your renovation funds are held in a MAS-regulated escrow account with DBS, in dedicated custodian accounts kept fully segregated from both Handshake's and your ID firm's own money — so they are never exposed to how either business happens to be doing. Every release is tied to a milestone agreed in writing before work starts, and nothing moves until you approve the completed stage. If something is disputed, the remaining funds stay exactly where they are: no party can access them unilaterally, and Handshake runs a structured resolution process — negotiation, mediation, arbitration — with the timelines and trade-offs set out in advance. It does not replace CASE or the Small Claims Tribunal if a dispute has to go that far. It means that when you get there, the money is still sitting in the account, rather than being the thing you are chasing.

How a milestone payment moves

Your ID invites you to the project

Every milestone and payment stage is set out and approved by you before any money moves.

You fund the milestone into escrow

Into a MAS-regulated DBS custodian account, segregated from everyone's operating cash — including Handshake's.

Your ID completes the stage

Hacking, plumbing and electrical, carpentry — whatever the milestone covers. Drawings, invoices and updates stay in one shared channel.

You inspect and approve

Only then is that milestone released. Everything else stays held.

A dispute freezes the money, not the project

Neither side can move the funds alone. Negotiation, mediation or arbitration follows, on timelines agreed up front.

⚑ This is Pillar 9 in your assessment. An ID who already works with escrow, or is willing to, scores the full 5 points. One who refuses outright scores zero — and it is worth asking why, given escrow is what guarantees they get paid. There is no licensing regime for interior design in Singapore, so how a firm is willing to handle your money is one of the few hard signals you get.
