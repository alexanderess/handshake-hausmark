Pillar 9 · Escrow payment

Why the way you pay matters as much as who you hire.

A renovation runs on money paid for work that does not exist yet. The standard schedule asks for a deposit at signing and progress payments at each stage, which means that at nearly every point in the project you are ahead of your contractor — you have funded work that has not been done. That arrangement is fine right up until it isn't. If the firm stalls, overruns, or winds up, the money has already left your account, and you join the queue of people trying to get it back.

Escrow changes the order without changing the deal. You commit the funds up front, so your ID has certainty they will be paid and can confidently order materials and book trades. But the money is held by a neutral third party rather than sitting in the firm's account, and it is released stage by stage as work is actually completed and you have signed it off. Neither side is holding the other's money. Your ID stops chasing payment, and you stop paying for a kitchen you cannot yet see.

Handshake Renovation Safeguard

Safeguard is the protection wrapped around those payments. Your money is held separately from the ID firm's own working capital, so it is not exposed to how that business happens to be doing. Every release is tied to a milestone that you and the firm agreed in writing before work started, and no release happens without your sign-off. If a milestone is disputed, the remaining funds stay where they are while it is worked through — so what keeps both sides at the table is the money itself, not goodwill. It also gives you a documented payment record, which is exactly what you need if a disagreement ever has to go further.

How a milestone payment moves

You and your ID agree the milestones

Written into the quotation before any money moves: what counts as done at each stage, and what it is worth.

You fund the escrow account

The full amount is committed, so your ID can order materials and book trades against real certainty of payment.

Your ID completes the stage

Hacking, plumbing and electrical, carpentry — whatever the agreed milestone covers.

You inspect and release

You sign off, and only that milestone's funds are released. The rest stays held.

Disputes pause the money, not the project

If a stage is not right, the remaining balance stays in escrow while it is resolved.

⚑ This is Pillar 9 in your assessment. An ID who already works with escrow, or is willing to, scores the full 5 points. One who refuses outright scores zero — and it is worth asking why, given escrow guarantees that they get paid.
