Pillar 9 · Escrow payment

Why the way you pay matters as much as who you hire.

Most renovation contracts in Singapore ask you to pay ahead of the work. A deposit on signing, then progress payments at each stage. Handshake's guidance is that no full home renovation needs a deposit above 20%, and that a fair schedule runs four to five stages with 10–15% upfront. Plenty of firms still ask for 30%, 40% or 50%. Whatever the split, you are paying for work nobody has done yet. If the firm stops work, runs badly over, or closes down, the money has already left your account and you are an unsecured creditor trying to get it back.

Renovation insurance will not help you here. It covers accidents and damage to the property. Deposit loss, contractor default, payment disputes and incomplete works sit outside every renovation insurance product sold in Singapore. Escrow is what covers those.

With escrow you still commit the full amount up front, so your ID can order materials and book trades knowing the money is there. What changes is where it sits. It goes into a regulated third-party account rather than the firm's own, and it moves across one stage at a time, after you have seen the work and approved it.

Handshake Renovation Safeguard

Your renovation funds sit in a MAS-regulated escrow account at DBS, in custodian accounts held separately from Handshake's money and from your ID firm's money. Each release is tied to a milestone the two of you agreed in writing before work started, and it happens only when you approve that stage. If you disagree about a stage, the balance stays where it is. Neither side can move it alone. Handshake then runs a dispute process covering negotiation, mediation and arbitration, with the timelines set out before you start.

If a dispute goes all the way to CASE, the Small Claims Tribunal or court, Safeguard will not settle it for you. But your money is still in the account while that runs its course, and you have a dated record of every payment and approval.

How a milestone payment moves

Your ID invites you to the project

Every milestone and payment stage is set out and approved by you before any money moves.

You fund the milestone into escrow

Into a MAS-regulated DBS custodian account, segregated from everyone's operating cash — including Handshake's.

Your ID completes the stage

Hacking, plumbing and electrical, carpentry — whatever the milestone covers. Drawings, invoices and updates stay in one shared channel.

You inspect and approve

Only then is that milestone released. Everything else stays held.

A dispute freezes the money, not the project

Neither side can move the funds alone. Negotiation, mediation or arbitration follows, on timelines agreed up front.
